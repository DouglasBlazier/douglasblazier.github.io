⚖️Reporting

The daily tonnage report catches shipments whose catch weights look wrong: weights that were averaged, keyed by hand or entered for the wrong pack unit. Two workflows turn a raw export into a color-coded, investigated report that's ready for review.

Every screenshot on this page is a recreation using made-up sample data. Customers, items, invoices and weights are invented.

Tonnage Report Formatter

The raw tonnage export is one long sheet of every weighed shipment. This workflow adds the math, pulls out the rows worth a second look and fixes a known pack-size issue on one product family.

How it runs

  1. At 9:00 AM it runs the ERP's tonnage report and exports it to Excel. On Mondays it covers Saturday through Monday, and missed days are added automatically.
  2. It adds two formula columns: Avg = Qty × Item Weight, and Var = Ext. Weight − Avg.
  3. A row is an outlier when the item is weight-tracked and |Var| is more than 20 lb.
  4. Mozzarella rows get a corrected item weight worked out from the pack size (for example 4/5 LB → 20 lb) before they're judged.
  5. It writes three tabs: 20up (outliers), Mozzarella (corrected rows) and Sheet1 (all the data with formulas).
Starts when
Automatically at 9:00 AM each weekday.
Produces
Tonnage_Report_M-D-YY.xlsx with 20up, Mozzarella and Sheet1 tabs.
Why it matters
Hundreds of rows become a short list worth checking.

Tonnage Report Check

For each outlier, this workflow opens the invoice in the ERP and reads every case weight, the way I would. It looks for signs of averaged or mistyped weights, finds the bills of lading, then color-codes and sorts the report using my rules.

How it runs

  1. For each 20up row, it opens the invoice in view-only mode with a fixed keyboard routine and reads every case weight.
  2. It looks for patterns: identical weights on two or more cases (a possible average), or a weight that matches one unit of the pack instead of a full case.
  3. For possible averages, it walks back through purchase history to find the bills of lading (BOLs) to check. Meat always gets at least two.
  4. It colors every row, adds bold notes under the rows that need them and leaves an empty reply row for my answer.
  5. It sorts by color group, then by variance, with a blank row between groups.
  6. After I review and recolor, it reorganizes my saved file and keeps my colors and notes.

Color rules

  • Red: weights confirmed wrong, after I check the BOLs
  • Yellow + note: possible average, single-unit weight, or an odd employee purchase
  • Green + FYI: scanned, but weights cluster tightly
  • Blue: beef trim or combo tote
  • Pink: meat-room cut or cut-to-order item
  • Gray: miscellaneous keyed-weight item
  • Green: every case scanned with varying weights, so it's fine
